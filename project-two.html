01The moment

Setting the stage

Dayout is an app where people share itineraries of places they've been, from trips to daily strolls and city walks. They set the stops and timing, and anyone can follow the same route.

To get more people posting, Dayout introduced a paid feature for creators: they can now sell their itineraries on the app. One initiative to launch it was an ad campaign, where I helped strategize the ad and created it, from scripting what to say to filming and editing.

02The goal

The problem

Objective: Drive creator sign-ups and let people know about the new paid feature.

The problem: As a startup, Dayout had almost no social media presence. No one knew what Dayout was, let alone the brand-new paid feature, and there was no consistent posting schedule on Instagram.

Funnel stage: Awareness → Conversion

07Adapting

What went wrong and how I adapted

How you responded, or what you changed once you noticed.

08Takeaways

What I learned

Repeat what works

Not everything needs to be a new idea. Constantly trying new things cost us followers and engagement; turning our best-performing topics into a series would have worked better.

Brand is more than content

I put most of my focus on reels and content and neglected the brand's visual design. More branded designs would have built a stronger, more recognizable visual voice.

09Next steps

If I were them…

  • Turn the best-performing topics into recurring series, so followers know what to expect.
  • Build a simple visual brand kit (colors, type, templates) so every post looks unmistakably Dayout.
